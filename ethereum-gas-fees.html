Ethereum Gas Fees Explained

A simple guide to gas fees: what gas is, why fees change, and how to pay less. For live network stats, open Ethereum Pulse.

What is “gas” on Ethereum?

Gas is the unit that measures the computational work required to process actions on Ethereum. Simple transfers use little gas, while complex smart contract interactions use more.

Why do gas fees go up and down?

Fees are mainly driven by demand for block space. When many users compete to get included in blocks, fees increase. When activity is lower, fees drop.

Base fee vs priority fee (simple version)

  • Base fee: adjusts with network demand and is required.
  • Priority fee (tip): extra incentive for validators to include your transaction faster.
  • Total fee: roughly base fee + priority fee × gas used.

How to pay less gas (practical tips)

  • Try off-peak times when the network is less busy.
  • Avoid urgent transactions if you can wait for lower fees.
  • Use Layer 2 networks when possible for cheaper execution.
  • Batch actions (e.g., approvals) when your wallet/app supports it.

FAQ

What are Ethereum gas fees?

Gas fees are transaction costs paid to use Ethereum (send ETH, swap tokens, interact with contracts).

Why do gas fees change?

Fees rise when the network is busy (high demand for block space) and fall when demand decreases.

How can I reduce gas fees?

Use off-peak hours, avoid urgency, and use Layer 2 networks for cheaper transactions.
